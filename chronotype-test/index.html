About this instrument

What this measures — and what it doesn't

  • Measures

    Morning–evening preference

    Where you sit on the chronotype spectrum, based on your natural sleep timing, morning alertness, and when your energy peaks. This reflects your internal body clock — real, partly heritable circadian science.

  • Method

    9-item preference scale

    Original questions inspired by the morningness–eveningness literature, each weighted toward morning or evening. Your total places you in one of five bands from Definite Evening to Definite Morning.

  • Result

    Chronotype + peak window

    Your chronotype, your position on the spectrum, your likely peak-focus window, and specific guidance on when to schedule demanding work versus routine tasks.

  • Limits

    Honest limits

    Self-report reflects how you see your rhythm, not always how your body behaves. It's a snapshot that shifts with stress and age, and it's never a sleep-disorder screen. Track your real energy to confirm.

Questions

Before you begin

What is a chronotype?

Your chronotype is your natural preference for when to sleep, wake, and be active — where you fall on the spectrum from strong morning type (Early Bird) to strong evening type (Night Owl). It reflects your internal body clock, which is partly genetic and shapes when you feel most alert.

How do I use my peak window?

Schedule your most demanding, focus-heavy work inside your peak window and protect it from meetings and distractions. Save routine, low-stakes tasks for your off-peak hours. Aligning hard work with your natural high point is one of the simplest evidence-backed ways to get more done with less strain.

Is this a medical or sleep-disorder test?

No. This is a self-reflection tool that estimates your morning–evening preference, not a clinical assessment. It can't diagnose insomnia, sleep apnea, or any disorder. If you regularly struggle to sleep or feel exhausted no matter your schedule, that's worth raising with a doctor.

Is my data saved anywhere?

No. The test runs entirely in your browser. Your answers never leave your device and nothing is stored or sent to a server. Refreshing the page clears everything.

Scoring & evidence

How the chronotype estimate is built

The chronotype tool uses nine original preference questions inspired by morningness–eveningness research without reproducing a proprietary clinical questionnaire. Each answer contributes a morningness value. The total is normalized between the minimum and maximum possible scores to a 0–100 morningness index and then mapped into descriptive bands with general peak-time guidance.

Because this is a preference scale, the result reflects your current self-reported schedule tendencies as well as biology. School/work obligations, season, age, light exposure, sleep debt, and habits can push behavior away from an underlying circadian preference.

The result cannot diagnose insomnia, delayed sleep-wake phase disorder, sleep apnea, or another sleep condition. It is a planning aid for thinking about when you tend to feel alert, not a medical assessment.

Interpretation standard: this result is designed for education and self-reflection. Unless a page explicitly documents representative norms and validation evidence, percentile-style or 1–99 outputs should be read as approximate internal references—not clinical, diagnostic, hiring, admissions, or credentialing scores.
