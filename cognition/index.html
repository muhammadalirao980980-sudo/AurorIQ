What gets measured

Five reasoning domains, one profile

Your composite score is built from distinct kinds of thinking. The balance between them — not the single number — is what defines your cognitive archetype.

  • Pattern

    Fluid reasoning

    Spotting the rule behind a sequence and predicting what comes next — the closest thing to raw fluid intelligence, and the least dependent on anything you were taught. Test your logical reasoning → · The science →

  • Numeric

    Quantitative reasoning

    Relationships between quantities: proportions, progressions, and structure in numbers. This is reasoning with numbers, not arithmetic recall — a calculator won't help.

  • Verbal

    Language & inference

    Analogies, meaning, and what follows from what. Verbal reasoning leans on crystallized knowledge more than the other domains, which is exactly why it's measured separately. Test your verbal reasoning →

  • Spatial

    Mental rotation & form

    Holding a shape in mind and transforming it — folding, rotating, recombining. Strong spatial profiles cluster in engineering, design, and the trades. Test your rotation → · The science →

  • Memory

    Working-memory span

    How much you can hold and manipulate at once — the mental workbench every other domain borrows. It's the domain most sensitive to sleep and stress. Test your span → · The science →

After the test

Reading your result honestly

A score without context is flattery. These are the two pages that turn your number into understanding.

One practical note before you begin: cognitive performance is state-dependent. Sleep debt, acute stress, and even time of day measurably move working-memory and processing scores, so a result from a rough night understates you. Test rested, test once, and treat the confidence interval — not the single number — as your actual result.

Questions

About this category

What does the Cognition category cover?

Instruments that measure reasoning ability: the flagship adaptive IQ test, plus memory-span, reaction-time, mental-rotation, verbal-reasoning, logical-reasoning, and creativity tests. Each reports honest, percentile-based estimates — and where a score can't be measured honestly, we say so.

Why does the IQ test measure five separate domains?

Because intelligence isn't one skill. Pattern, numeric, verbal, spatial, and memory reasoning draw on different cognitive machinery, and the balance between them — not just the composite score — is what defines your cognitive archetype.

Is a high score in one domain and a low score in another normal?

Completely. Uneven profiles are the norm, not the exception. A strong spatial score with weaker verbal reasoning, or the reverse, simply describes the shape of your mind — it's the reason two people with identical composite scores can be entirely different thinkers.
