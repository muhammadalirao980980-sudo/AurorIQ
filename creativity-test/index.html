    About this instrument

    What this measures — and what it doesn't

    • Measures

      Divergent thinking

      The ability to branch outward from one starting point and generate many possibilities — the ideation phase of creative work. The Alternate Uses Task is its most established laboratory measure.

    • Method

      Alternate Uses, 2 minutes

      One common object, a two-minute clock, and free text — one idea per line. Repeated or reworded ideas collapse to one, so padding the list doesn't help. Fluency and variety are computed live and on submit.

    • Result

      Fluency, variety, percentile

      A count of distinct ideas, a count of distinct approaches, and an approximate fluency percentile. Originality is discussed but never scored — because faking a number we can't compute would betray the whole point.

    • Limits

      Honest limits

      Fluency rewards quantity over quality, and divergent thinking is only one slice of creativity. This is the least-validated measure on the platform, and we'd rather say that than oversell it. It's a warm-up, not an assessment.

    Questions

    Before you begin

    What does this creativity test measure?

    Divergent thinking — your ability to generate many different ideas from one starting point — using the Alternate Uses Task. You list as many uses as you can for a common object within two minutes. It measures idea fluency and variety, which is one facet of creativity, not the whole of it.

    Why don't you score originality?

    Because we can't do it honestly. Real originality scoring compares each idea against how rare it is among thousands of other people's answers, which needs a large database or human judgement. This test runs entirely in your browser, so instead of faking an originality number, we measure what we genuinely can — fluency and variety — and say so plainly.

    What counts as a good fluency score?

    For a common object in two minutes, adults typically produce around eight to twelve distinct uses. We place your count against roughly that range. But fluency rewards quantity, not quality — ten obvious ideas score higher than three brilliant ones, which is exactly the limitation of measuring divergent thinking by counting.

    Is a low score a sign I'm not creative?

    Not at all. Divergent-thinking fluency is a narrow slice of creativity and correlates only modestly with real-world creative achievement, which also depends on deep knowledge, persistence, and craft. Treat this as a fun warm-up for idea generation, not a verdict on your creative worth.
