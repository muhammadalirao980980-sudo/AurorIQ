About this instrument

What this measures — and what it doesn't

  • Measures

    Effective reading speed

    Words per minute on general prose, gated by whether you actually absorbed it. Raw speed alone is easy to fake; effective speed — pace weighted by comprehension — is the number that means something.

  • Method

    Timed passage + 5 questions

    An original ~240-word passage is timed from reveal to your "done" press. Five multiple-choice questions then test recall. Score below 60% and the percentile is withheld; read implausibly fast and the run is voided.

  • Result

    Raw + effective WPM, percentile

    You see raw pace, effective pace, comprehension, and — when earned — a percentile against approximate adult norms (~238 WPM average). The two speeds together tell you whether to push faster or slow down.

  • Limits

    Honest limits

    Speed depends heavily on the material — dense or unfamiliar text reads slower for everyone. This isn't a literacy or dyslexia assessment, and a single passage is a rough snapshot. It's a mirror for your own habits, not a verdict.

Before you begin

How is my reading speed measured?

You read a short original passage at your natural pace, then answer five comprehension questions. Your raw speed is the passage's word count divided by the time you spent reading, expressed in words per minute (WPM).

Why does comprehension affect my score?

Because reading faster than you understand isn't reading faster — it's skimming. If you score below 60% on the questions, the percentile is withheld. Your effective speed scales raw pace by comprehension, so fast-but-shallow reading is penalised in proportion.

What is a typical adult reading speed?

Silent reading of general prose averages around 238 words per minute for adults, with a wide spread. We place your effective speed against approximately N(238, 68), so ~238 WPM sits near the 50th percentile and ~300 WPM near the 80th.

Are speed-reading claims of 1,000+ WPM real?

Not with full comprehension of new material. Above roughly 400–500 WPM, comprehension of unfamiliar text drops sharply — what's marketed as speed reading is usually skimming, which has its place but isn't the same thing. This test is built to tell the difference.
