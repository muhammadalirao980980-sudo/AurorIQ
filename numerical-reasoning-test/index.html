About this instrument

What this measures — and what it doesn't

  • Measures

    Quantitative reasoning

    Your ability to reason with numbers across four kinds of problem — number series, arithmetic word problems, ratios and percentages, and data interpretation — the third leg of reasoning beside verbal and logical.

  • Method

    15 items, weighted

    Five questions from each of three difficulty tiers, drawn from a larger bank and shuffled. Harder items count for more, and your weighted accuracy maps to an internal score index.

  • Result

    Internal index + breakdown

    An internal score index plus your accuracy and how you did at each tier. Near-chance results are withheld rather than shown, because a guess-level score would mislead more than inform.

  • Limits

    Honest limits

    Numerical reasoning leans on math schooling, so it isn't culture- or education-fair like abstract logic. A short test is a snapshot, not a diagnosis, and can't be used for clinical or selection decisions.

Questions

Before you begin

What is numerical reasoning?

Numerical reasoning is the ability to work with numbers and quantitative relationships — spotting patterns in sequences, solving word problems, handling ratios and percentages, and drawing conclusions from data. It's one of the core reasoning abilities, alongside verbal and logical reasoning, and it's exactly what many employers assess in aptitude tests.

Is this test culture-fair like the logical test?

Less so, and we won't pretend otherwise. Because numerical reasoning draws on math you learned in school, your results reflect familiarity with arithmetic and percentages as well as raw reasoning. That makes it useful and job-relevant, but not education-fair the way abstract pattern logic is.

How is the score calculated?

You answer fifteen questions spread across three difficulty tiers, and harder items count for more. Your weighted accuracy is converted to an internal score index. If your accuracy is near chance for four-choice questions, the score is withheld as unreliable rather than shown.

Is my data saved anywhere?

No. The entire test runs in your browser. Your answers never leave your device and nothing is stored or sent to a server. Refreshing the page clears everything.

Scoring & evidence

How the numerical score is built

The test samples 15 questions across easy, medium, and hard tiers, with five items from each tier. Correct answers receive difficulty weights of 1, 2, and 3. The weighted proportion is adjusted above the 25% guessing level for four-choice items and mapped to an approximate 1–99 composite; raw accuracy at or below 30% triggers a reliability warning and withholds the internal index display.

This is deliberately not described as a pure measure of general intelligence. Numerical reasoning depends on arithmetic fluency, ratios, percentages, and prior schooling as well as novel problem solving. A lower result can therefore reflect unfamiliarity with the mathematical language used by the items, while a higher result can reflect practiced quantitative skills. The internal composite has not been established as a representative population percentile.

Interpret the score alongside the tier breakdown: consistent performance across difficulty levels is more informative than a single internal score. The assessment logic runs locally in the browser in the current codebase; advertising and analytics do not enter the scoring formula.

Interpretation standard: this result is designed for education and self-reflection. Unless a page explicitly documents representative norms and validation evidence, percentile-style or 1–99 outputs should be read as approximate internal references—not clinical, diagnostic, hiring, admissions, or credentialing scores.
