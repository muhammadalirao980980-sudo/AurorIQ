About this instrument

What this measures — and what it doesn't

  • Measures

    Crystallized verbal ability

    How well you grasp relationships between words: analogies, shades of meaning, opposites. Unlike fluid reasoning, this draws on knowledge you've built — which is why verbal scores keep rising with reading across a lifetime.

  • Method

    15 graded items, weighted

    Five easy, five medium, five hard, drawn from an authored bank and presented easiest-first. Answer options are shuffled each run, and the score weights each item by difficulty so a hard win counts triple an easy one.

  • Result

    Internal index + tier breakdown

    You get an internal score index plus how you did at each difficulty — useful for seeing whether you plateau at medium or push into the hard tier. Near-chance accuracy is flagged and the score withheld.

  • Limits

    Honest limits

    This is an English-vocabulary test, so it reflects language exposure as much as reasoning — non-native speakers are genuinely disadvantaged, and we say so. Fifteen items is a snapshot, not a normed instrument, and never an aptitude credential.

Scoring & evidence

How the verbal score is built

Fifteen items are sampled evenly from three difficulty tiers. Easy, medium, and hard correct answers carry weights of 1, 2, and 3, then the weighted total is adjusted above the 25% chance level for four-option items and converted to an approximate 1–99 composite. Results are withheld when raw accuracy is 30% or below because a near-chance response pattern is not a stable basis for interpretation.

The score reflects performance on English analogies, synonyms, antonyms, and related verbal relationships. That means language exposure and reading history are part of the construct: this is not language-free reasoning. A multilingual user or someone who has learned English recently may show lower performance without having lower general reasoning ability.

The result is an educational indicator for this item bank, not a clinically normed verbal-IQ score. The browser calculates it locally; no individual answer-upload call is present in the current verbal tool script.

Interpretation standard: this result is designed for education and self-reflection. Unless a page explicitly documents representative norms and validation evidence, percentile-style or 1–99 outputs should be read as approximate internal references—not clinical, diagnostic, hiring, admissions, or credentialing scores. Questions

Before you begin

What does a verbal reasoning test measure?

Your grasp of relationships between words and ideas — through analogies, synonyms, and antonyms. It leans on crystallized intelligence: the vocabulary and verbal knowledge you accumulate through reading and experience, rather than raw processing speed.

Why are harder questions worth more?

Getting an easy item right tells us less than nailing a hard one. The score weights each item by difficulty, so your result reflects the ceiling of your verbal ability, not just how many gentle questions you cleared.

Does this test disadvantage non-native English speakers?

Honestly, somewhat — any English vocabulary test does. Verbal reasoning is inseparable from language exposure, so a lower score can reflect how long you've read in English rather than reasoning ability itself. We flag near-chance results and say this plainly in your results.

Can verbal reasoning improve?

Yes, more than most cognitive skills. Crystallized abilities like vocabulary grow across the whole lifespan through reading, so verbal scores tend to rise with age and exposure. Wide reading is the single most reliable way to improve.
