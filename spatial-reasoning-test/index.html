Cognition · ~4 minutes

Turn it over in your mind's eye.

Two shapes, twelve times: is the second one the first simply rotated — or is it a mirror image that no amount of turning will match? This is mental rotation, the most studied measure of spatial ability, scored on accuracy and speed.

12 shapes · Accuracy + speed · On-device

You'll see a reference shape on the left and a candidate on the right. Decide whether the candidate is the same shape rotated, or a mirror image. Work quickly but carefully — the test tracks how long each decision takes, because speed is part of spatial skill. There's no going back once you answer.

About this instrument

What this measures — and what it doesn't

  • Measures

    Mental rotation

    Spatial visualization: holding a shape in mind and turning it to compare against another. It's the spatial skill with the strongest links to engineering, design, surgery, and skilled trades — and one of the clearest windows into spatial reasoning.

  • Method

    Same-or-mirror, 12 items

    Each candidate is generated as a genuine rotation — or reflection — of the reference, using shapes chosen so the two can never be confused. Every item has an exact right answer computed in geometry, not eyeballed, so your score reflects the real distinction.

  • Result

    Accuracy, speed, percentile

    You see how many you got right, your median decision time, and an approximate percentile read against the 50% chance floor. Accuracy near chance is flagged and the score withheld — guessing shouldn't look like ability.

  • Limits

    Honest limits

    Twelve two-choice items is a snapshot, not a full spatial battery, and screen size and pointer precision play a small role. This isn't a clinical or aptitude credential — but spatial skill is trainable, so treat a low score as a baseline you can move.

Questions

Before you begin

What does a mental rotation test measure?

Spatial visualization — your ability to picture an object turning in your mind and compare it to another. It's one of the most studied and job-relevant spatial skills, linked to performance in engineering, design, surgery, and the trades.

How do I tell 'same' from 'mirror'?

A 'same' shape can be turned to match the reference exactly. A 'mirror' shape is flipped — no amount of rotation will line it up, because it's the reference's reflection. Every shape here is chosen so that this distinction is unambiguous.

Why does the test track my speed?

Classic research found that the time to decide rises steadily with the angle of rotation — strong evidence that people really do rotate a mental image, step by step. Faster accurate decisions suggest more fluent spatial visualization, so speed is reported alongside accuracy.

Can spatial skills improve with practice?

Yes — spatial reasoning is among the more trainable cognitive skills. Practice with rotation puzzles, construction, drawing, and certain video games measurably improves it. A single run here is a snapshot; the value is in noticing where you start and whether it moves.
