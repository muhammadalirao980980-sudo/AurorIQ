    Questions

    Before you begin

    What is a time audit?

    A time audit is a simple accounting of how you spend the 168 hours in a week. You estimate your hours across categories like sleep, work, commuting, and leisure, and the totals reveal the real shape of your time — often quite different from how it feels day to day.

    How accurate are my estimates?

    Probably less than you'd think — people are famously bad at estimating their own time, usually overestimating work and underestimating screens. That's fine: even a rough estimate reveals the overall shape. For a precise picture, track your actual hours for a week and come back.

    Does this tell me how I should spend my time?

    No. There's no correct allocation, and the observations are descriptive, not prescriptive — the one exception is sleep, where the ~7–9 hour range is well established. Beyond that, this is a mirror, not a rulebook. What a good week looks like is entirely yours to decide.

    Is my data saved anywhere?

    No. Everything runs in your browser. Your entries never leave your device and nothing is stored or sent to a server. Refreshing the page clears everything.

    Method & limits

    How the weekly analysis is produced

    The Time Audit is an allocation tool rather than a psychometric test. You distribute the fixed 168 hours in a week across categories such as sleep, work or study, commuting, care, social time, leisure, and hobbies. The analysis converts each category to a share of the week, checks whether the total fits inside 168 hours, and derives summaries such as sleep per night, discretionary time, and the share of waking hours devoted to work or study.

    The observations are rule-based prompts, not a productivity score or diagnosis. For example, the code can flag very low or high sleep allocations and show which non-sleep category occupies the largest share. It does not know whether a particular allocation is appropriate for your health, responsibilities, culture, disability, job, or family situation.

    The value of the tool is visibility: it makes trade-offs explicit so you can decide what to change. Treat its observations as questions for reflection, not as an instruction to cut sleep, care responsibilities, school, or other essential needs.

    Interpretation standard: this tool is educational and planning-oriented. Its internal percentages, bands, or observations are not clinical measures or representative population percentiles.
