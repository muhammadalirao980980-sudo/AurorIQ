About this instrument

What this measures — and what it doesn't

  • Measures

    Working preferences

    Where you sit on six preference spectrums — collaboration, structure, focus, decision-making, pace, and environment. Preferences for how you operate, not measures of how well you perform.

  • Method

    24-item bipolar scale

    Four statements per spectrum, half worded toward each end to counter the pull to just agree. Your answers place a marker along each spectrum, with a real "balanced" zone in the middle.

  • Result

    Six spectrums + tips

    A visual position on each spectrum, the side you lean toward (or balanced), and a practical tip for making the most of that preference — no type, no label, no ranking against anyone.

  • Limits

    Honest limits

    Self-report captures how you see yourself, which shifts with role, team, and mood. It says nothing about ability or fit for a specific job. It's a mirror for self-awareness, not a type or a hiring tool.

Questions

Before you begin

Is one work style better than another?

No. Every spectrum here is a preference, not an ability — an independent worker isn't better or worse than a collaborative one, just different. What matters is fit between how you prefer to work and what a role or team asks of you. There's no winning profile.

How is this different from a personality type?

It deliberately avoids putting you in a box. Instead of a four-letter type, you get a position on each spectrum, including a genuine 'balanced' result when you don't lean strongly. Types feel satisfying but oversimplify; a spectrum shows the nuance that a label throws away.

Will my results always be the same?

Not necessarily. Preferences shift with context — a demanding role, a new team, or just your mood that day can move where you land. This captures how you see yourself right now, which is useful for reflection, not a fixed trait carved in stone.

Is my data saved anywhere?

No. The entire test runs in your browser. Your answers never leave your device and nothing is stored or sent to a server. Refreshing the page clears everything.

Scoring & evidence

How the work-style profile is built

The work-style inventory scores several two-pole dimensions. Each 1–5 agreement response is aligned toward one side of its dimension; items written toward the opposite pole are reverse-aligned before averaging. The result is converted to a 0–100 position for each spectrum.

Scores at or below 40 lean toward one pole, scores at or above 60 lean toward the other, and the middle range is reported as balanced. These cut points are descriptive rules in the AurorIQ tool, not clinical norms or population percentiles.

A work-style preference is contextual. People can behave differently across teams, roles, stress levels, cultures, and stages of a project. Use the profile to name preferences and trade-offs, not to decide that a person is incapable of working in a different style.

Interpretation standard: this result is designed for education and self-reflection. Unless a page explicitly documents representative norms and validation evidence, percentile-style or 1–99 outputs should be read as approximate internal references—not clinical, diagnostic, hiring, admissions, or credentialing scores.
