About this instrument

What this measures — and what it doesn't

  • Measures

    Working-memory span

    How many items you can hold and manipulate at once — the mental workbench that pattern, numeric, and verbal reasoning all borrow. Forward span isolates storage; backward span adds executive control.

  • Method

    Classic digit span

    Digits at a fixed ~0.9-second cadence, two trials per length, phase ends after two failures at one length. Forward starts at three digits, backward at two — the standard clinical-style ladder, self-administered.

  • Result

    Span + approximate percentile

    Your spans are placed against internal illustrative reference values (~6.6 forward, ~4.9 backward). The tilde is deliberate: single-session span is a noisier estimate than a full adaptive battery, and we report it that way.

  • Limits

    Honest limits

    Not a clinical memory assessment and not a dementia screen. Span is state-dependent — sleep debt, stress, and noise lower it. An unusual result on one day means very little; a concern about real-life memory belongs with a clinician.

Scoring & evidence

How the working-memory score is built

The task estimates forward and backward digit span separately. Sequences increase in length, with two trials available at a given length, up to the tool's maximum span. Forward span mainly reflects short-term maintenance, while backward span adds a manipulation requirement because the sequence must be mentally reversed.

The current scoring code converts forward and backward spans to z-scores using built-in approximate reference means and standard deviations, then combines those standardized values into a composite percentile-style result. These embedded reference values are useful for orientation but are not a substitute for age-stratified norms collected specifically for AurorIQ under controlled conditions.

Working-memory span is sensitive to distraction, rehearsal strategies, hearing/reading conditions, and familiarity with digit-span tasks. Use the result as a snapshot of this session rather than a diagnosis or a fixed capacity limit.

Interpretation standard: this result is designed for education and self-reflection. Unless a page explicitly documents representative norms and validation evidence, percentile-style or 1–99 outputs should be read as approximate internal references—not clinical, diagnostic, hiring, admissions, or credentialing scores. Questions

Before you begin

What does this memory test measure?

Working-memory span — how many items you can hold and manipulate in mind at once — using the classic digit-span paradigm: digits are shown one at a time, and you recall them in order (forward) and then in reverse order (backward).

How is my span scored?

You get two trials at each length. Recalling at least one correctly moves you up a digit; failing both ends that phase. Your span is the longest length you recalled, compared against internal illustrative reference values — around 6–7 digits forward and 4–5 backward for most adults.

Why is backward span harder — and measured separately?

Forward span mostly measures short-term storage. Reversing the digits forces you to store and transform at the same time, which leans on executive control — the part of working memory that predicts reasoning ability best. That's why the two phases are scored separately before being combined.

Is this a clinical memory assessment?

No. This is an educational instrument, and the percentiles are illustrative comparisons with internal reference values — useful for self-reflection, not diagnosis. Span is also state-dependent: sleep debt and stress measurably lower it, so test rested. If you have real concerns about your memory, speak with a clinician.

How the comparison is calculated

Your longest successful forward and backward spans are the main results. The additional percentile-style number uses built-in reference means of 6.6 and 4.9 digits, and standard deviations of 1.1 and 1.2. These constants do not have a verified source and representative sample documented for this implementation. Treat the comparison as an illustration, not your rank among adults.

The tool standardizes each span, averages those values and applies a normal-distribution conversion. Averaging two standardized scores does not by itself establish a population percentile. Compare raw spans under similar conditions instead.
