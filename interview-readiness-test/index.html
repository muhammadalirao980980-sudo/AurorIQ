About this instrument

What this measures — and what it doesn't

  • Measures

    Practical preparation

    How ready you are for a specific interview across five factors: research, stories, questions, logistics, and rehearsal. Not a trait, type, or aptitude — a checklist of what's within your control.

  • Method

    20 statements, weighted

    Four statements per factor, half describing readiness and half describing a gap, reverse-scored so agreeing with either direction is scored correctly.

  • Result

    A score plus your top fixes

    An overall readiness score, a breakdown by factor, and your two lowest-scoring factors with a concrete fix for each — the two changes most likely to matter before your interview.

  • Limits

    Honest limits

    This measures preparation, not talent or fit, and can't predict an outcome that also depends on the employer, the competition, and factors outside your control. It's a mirror on readiness, not a forecast.

Questions

Before you begin

Is this a personality test?

No, deliberately not. This checks how prepared you are for a specific upcoming interview — your research, stories, questions, logistics, and rehearsal — not a trait or type about who you are. Preparation is something you can change this week, which is exactly the point.

Will a high score guarantee I get the job?

No. A high score means you're well prepared, which meaningfully improves how you'll perform — but outcomes also depend on fit, competition, and factors entirely outside your control. Treat this as removing avoidable weaknesses, not as a prediction of the result.

What are the five factors it checks?

Research (do you understand the role and company), Stories (do you have concrete examples ready), Questions (do you have real questions for them), Logistics (is the practical setup handled), and Rehearsal (have you practiced out loud). Each is scored from twenty statements, four per factor.

Is my data saved anywhere?

No. The entire check runs in your browser. Your answers never leave your device and nothing is stored or sent to a server. Refreshing the page clears everything.

Scoring & evidence

How the interview-readiness score is built

The readiness check covers five practical dimensions: company/role research, prepared evidence stories, questions for the interviewer, logistics, and rehearsal. Each dimension has four 1–5 agreement items. Statements that describe weak preparation are reverse-scored so agreement correctly lowers the relevant dimension.

Each dimension is converted to 0–100, and the overall readiness score is the mean of the five dimension scores. The resulting band is a preparation checklist, not a probability of receiving an offer. The code also identifies weaker areas so the output can point to specific actions before an interview.

Hiring outcomes depend on far more than preparation, including role fit, experience, interviewer judgment, competition, labor-market conditions, and bias. A high readiness score means your reported preparation is strong on this checklist; it does not guarantee selection.

Interpretation standard: this result is designed for education and self-reflection. Unless a page explicitly documents representative norms and validation evidence, percentile-style or 1–99 outputs should be read as approximate internal references—not clinical, diagnostic, hiring, admissions, or credentialing scores.
